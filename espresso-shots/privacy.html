Privacy Policy

Espresso Shots: Rude Awakening · Last updated 31 October 2026

Espresso Shots: Rude Awakening (“the game”) is made by Melvin Hung (“we”, “us”). This page explains what the game collects, why, who else handles it, and the choices you have.

The short version

  • You don’t need an account to play. We never ask for your name, email or contacts, and the game never asks for your location.
  • Your progress is saved on your iPhone (and in your own iCloud).
  • The game sends anonymous play data, like which levels you play and where the game crashes, so we can balance it and fix bugs. You can turn it off in Settings → Share play data.
  • Ads only play when you choose to watch one, and you decide whether they’re personalised.
  • Payments go through Apple. We never see your payment details.
  • We don’t sell your personal information.

1. Your save

Your progress, settings and a record of what you’ve bought are stored on your iPhone. The game also keeps a copy in the app’s files on your iPhone, which your iPhone’s own backups include, and a copy in your iCloud account, so your progress can come back after you reinstall the game or move to a new iPhone. The iCloud copy belongs to your Apple Account; we can’t read it. Deleting the game removes the copies on your iPhone. Settings → Reset progress erases your progress (but not your purchases).

2. Play data (PostHog)

To understand how the game is played, balance it and fix crashes, the game sends play data to PostHog, an analytics service that stores it for us in the United States.

What it includes

  • What happens in the game: shifts started and finished, the choices you’re offered and the ones you pick, rooms cleared, which screens you open and for how long, in-game cash and Beans earned and spent, purchases (which product, and whether it went through) and ads watched.
  • Crash and error reports, and how long the game takes to load.
  • A random ID the game creates the first time it runs, and an ID for each play session. Neither is linked to your name, your Apple Account or your device’s advertising ID.
  • Basic technical details: the game’s version, whether it came from the App Store or TestFlight, your iOS version, your screen size, and your phone’s language and region settings (such as “English, United States”). The region is a setting on your phone, not your location.

What it never includes

Your name, email, contacts, location, advertising ID or device model. We have set PostHog to discard IP addresses and not to work out where you are.

Turning it off

Settings → Share play data turns it off. The game then stops sending play data and deletes anything still waiting on your iPhone to be sent. You can turn it on again at any time.

When it starts, the game also checks with PostHog for an update notice from us (for example “a new version is out”). That check carries nothing that identifies you or your play, so it happens even when play data is off.

3. Ads (Google AdMob)

The game has optional ads: you choose to watch one, for example to double a reward. The ads are served by Google AdMob. Before the first ad, the game asks for your choice:

  • In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, Google shows its consent form first. You can change your answer later in Settings → Ad privacy choices.
  • Then iPhone’s own prompt asks whether the game may track you. If you allow it, Google may use your device’s advertising identifier to show ads based on your interests. If you don’t, the ads aren’t personalised. You can change this at any time in your iPhone’s Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Either way, Google receives information it needs to show an ad, measure it and prevent fraud, such as your IP address (which it may use to estimate your general area, such as your city), device information and how you interact with the ad. Google explains this in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services. The game asks for ads suitable for a general audience (rated PG at most).

4. Purchases (Apple and RevenueCat)

In-app purchases are handled by Apple, under Apple’s Privacy Policy. We never see your name, Apple Account or payment details. The game uses RevenueCat to check purchase receipts with Apple and to keep a record of what you’ve bought, so Restore purchases in the Shop can bring purchases back. That record is kept under a random ID RevenueCat creates, not under your name or email.

5. Game Center (Apple)

If you’re signed in to Game Center, the game reports your achievements and scores to it. Your Game Center nickname and scores can appear on leaderboards that other players see, depending on your Game Center settings. Game Center is run by Apple under Apple’s Privacy Policy.

6. Who gets your data

Only the services named above (PostHog, Google AdMob, RevenueCat and Apple), and only for the purposes described here. We don’t sell your personal information. If you allow tracking, Google may use your advertising identifier for personalised ads, as described in section 3; you can say no, or change your mind later.

7. How long we keep it

We keep play data only as long as we need it to improve the game and fix problems, then delete it. Google, RevenueCat and Apple keep data under their own policies, linked above.

8. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object to its use. In the EU and UK, we rely on our legitimate interest in improving the game for play data; you can object at any time by turning off Share play data. Because play data is kept under a random ID rather than your name or email, we usually can’t look it up from your name or email. Write to us at the address below and we’ll help as far as we can. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

9. Children

The game isn’t made for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect personal information from them. If you think a child has used the game and data about them has been collected, write to us and we’ll delete what we can.

10. Changes

If this policy changes, we’ll update this page and the date at the top. If a change is significant, we’ll also say so in the game.
